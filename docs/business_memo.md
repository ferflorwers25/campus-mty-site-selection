# Business memo: a stationery & print shop south of Tec de Monterrey

**To:** Entrepreneur (simulated client) · **From:** Fernando Flores, Data Analyst · **Date:** October 2026
**Versión en español:** [memo_negocio.md](memo_negocio.md)

## Recommendation

Open a **combined stationery + copy/print shop 700–800 m south / south-west of Campus Monterrey**, inside the three top-ranked areas below. **Before signing a lease**, run the 2-week field validation at the end of this memo.

## Why

| Evidence | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stationery shops within 1 km of campus vs. metro share | **6 shops · 0.37×** (under-supplied) | DENUE 2026 |
| Copy/print shops within 1 km vs. metro share | **4 shops · 0.58×** (under-supplied) | DENUE 2026 |
| Same categories around UANL Ciudad Universitaria | **1.74× and 5.46×** (over-supplied) | DENUE 2026 |
| Share of residents aged 18–24 around the top 3 areas | **23–25%** vs. **12.1%** in Nuevo León | Census 2020 |
| Residents aged 18–24 within a 6-minute walk of each top area | **1,170–1,300** | Census 2020 |
| Stationery/copy shops within that 6-minute walk | **0** | DENUE 2026 |

The gap is specific to the Tec, not to universities in general: around UANL the same businesses are over-represented. All 10 existing competitors within 1 km are micro-businesses (0–5 employees), so there is no dominant chain to fight.

**What to avoid:** laundromats (31 within 1 km, 7.1× the metro share), restaurants and cafés (~2×). These are saturated.

## Risks and how to reduce them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| On-campus print services may already cover demand (not listed in DENUE) | Visit campus print points; note prices and queue times at peak hours |
| Students print less than before (digital coursework) | Lead with what can't be digital: plotter/large-format, binding, model-making and architecture supplies |
| The Census counts residents, not students commuting through | Count foot traffic at each candidate block (see validation plan) |
| DENUE misses informal sellers | Walk a 500 m radius around each candidate and record every competitor |
| Rent near campus may be high | Compare rents in the 3 areas; a small storefront (20–40 m²) is enough |

## Validation plan (2 weeks, before any investment)

1. **Foot traffic:** count pedestrians for 15 minutes at 8:00, 13:00 and 18:00 on 3 weekdays at each candidate block.
2. **Competitor walk:** visit the 10 nearby competitors. Record prices, opening hours, services and peak-hour queues.
3. **Demand check:** 30 short student interviews. How often do they print or buy supplies, where, and what is missing?
4. **Rent survey:** available storefronts and asking rents in the 3 areas.
5. **Go / no-go:** go ahead if traffic is high **and** students report unmet needs that on-campus services don't cover.

## Method in one line

INEGI DENUE (173,829 businesses in the Monterrey metro) → competition by distance and location quotients → 2020 Census population by AGEB, interpolated onto a 250 m grid → score = residents aged 18–24 within 500 m ÷ (competitors + 1). Full analysis: [README](../README.md).

*Limitations: DENUE lists registered establishments only; Census data is from 2020; masked small counts slightly under-estimate demand; the score measures relative opportunity, not profit.*
